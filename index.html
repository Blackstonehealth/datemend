Datemend

Put the dates, places and captions back on your Snapchat Memories export.

Datemend is a free preview tool that runs in your web browser. You give it the mydata~…zip file(s) you downloaded from Snapchat; it writes each Memory's capture date and (when your export has coordinates) location back into the photo or video, merges photo captions and stickers, sorts everything into year/month folders, and gives you a report of what it did and what it could not do.

Is Snapchat deleting my Memories?

Some third-party sites have claimed Memories will be deleted on specific dates. Snapchat's own help page says otherwise. This is the wording as we read it on October 18, 2026, from help.snapchat.com: “How do I manage my Memories storage?”:

Will Snapchat delete my Memories if I do not upgrade?
No. Snapchat will not automatically delete your Memories because you do not upgrade.

Starting in January 2027, Memories that are more than one year old and are not part of your oldest 5GB of Memories will be archived and remain stored on Snapchat. You will continue to have access to Memories saved within the last year, as well as your oldest 5GB of Memories.

Archived Memories will appear as thumbnails in the app, but you will need to upgrade your storage to open, edit, or share them. You will have the options of subscribing or downloading your Memories to access them outside the app.

Snap can change that page. Check it yourself; our copy of the text is only a snapshot from the date above. Snap lists a 100GB Memories-only plan for $1.99/month or $20/year in the US (“Prices may vary”). Whether you pay Snap, download your Memories, or both is your call. Datemend only helps with the “download” route: a Snapchat export on its own tends to lose the context that makes photos usable in a photo library.

Because Snapchat's export has no per-item ID, Datemend pairs files with entries by day and photo/video type. When several Memories share a day it flags them as “ambiguous”, and it never writes GPS for an ambiguous pairing.

How to use it

  1. Request your data from Snapchat. Per Snap's help article “How do I download my data from Snapchat?” (read October 18, 2026): go to accounts.snapchat.com, open My Data, switch on “Export your Memories” (and choose “Request Only Memories” if you only want Memories). The article says “We strive to deliver data within 7 days, but large downloads can take longer” and “Try downloading your data from a computer rather than a mobile device.” Third-party reports say the date/location file (json/memories_history.json) only appears if you also tick the option to export JSON files, so tick it if you see it. Do not tick location history or chats unless you want them.
  2. Download every zip part (mydata~…zip, -2.zip, …). Do not rename or extract them.
  3. Open Datemend in desktop Chrome (the only browser we have tested; Edge should work but is untested), drop the zip file(s), read the “What was found” summary, pick a time zone and output, and run it.
  4. Open report.html in the result. Check counts of matched, ambiguous, unmatched and failed items, then spot-check a few files in your photo app before deleting anything.

Datemend never asks for your Snapchat username or password, never connects to Snapchat, and never opens the download links that may appear inside your export.

Privacy: your files stay on your computer

Your photos, videos and export data are read by your browser on your own device and are not uploaded anywhere. The Datemend page ships with a security policy (connect-src 'none') that tells your browser to block it from making any network connection, and its code contains no analytics, trackers, cookies, local storage or fetch calls. Automated tests check this, including a headless-browser run that confirmed the policy is enforced.

What we receive: nothing. There is no account, no sign-up, no server of ours, and no payment. Datemend does not log what is inside your files.

What our host sees: this site is hosted on GitHub Pages. When you load a page, GitHub, like any web host, receives your browser's request (for example your IP address) under GitHub's privacy statement. That happens when the page loads, before you choose any file.

How you can check: open your browser's developer tools, watch the Network tab while you process a sample, and you should see no requests after the page has loaded, other than any download you click. You can also take the page offline: download the offline copy below, or switch off your internet after the page loads: it is designed to keep working offline, but we have not tested that in every browser.

Your report is generated on your device and may contain your file names and locations. Treat it as private. Do not paste it, or any file from your export, into a public issue or forum.

Questions

Is this free?

The preview is free to use. There is no checkout and nothing to buy. If a paid version is added later, this page will say so plainly and will not imply you already paid for anything.

Do you upload my photos?

No. See the privacy section above. The page is built so that your browser blocks it from sending data anywhere.

Will my original export be changed?

No. Datemend reads your zip files and writes new files to the place you choose. Your downloaded export is not modified.

My photos still show the wrong date in my photo app.

Read report.html first: it lists items with no date, items it could not pair, and items it left unchanged on purpose (WebP/HEIC photos, fragmented videos). Then check the time-zone choice. Third-party reports say Snapchat stores times in UTC; the zone you pick decides the local time written into each file. Some apps cache old dates until you re-import into a fresh library.

Why is there no location on some Memories?

Either your export has no coordinates for them (some reports describe place names only, which Datemend does not convert), or several Memories shared a day and the pairing was ambiguous. In both cases Datemend leaves location out and tells you in the report rather than guess.

Why aren't my video captions burned in?

That requires re-encoding video, which this preview does not do. The overlay is saved as a separate image next to the video.

Is it OK to use a tool like this with Snapchat's terms?

We are not lawyers and make no claim either way. Datemend only works on a file you downloaded yourself through Snapchat's own My Data feature, on your own device. It never logs in to Snapchat or contacts it. Snap's Terms of Service are between you and Snap; read them if you have concerns.

Whose Memories can I process?

Your own, or someone's who has asked you to. Do not use it on someone else's data without their permission.

Do I get a refund?

There is nothing to refund: the preview is free and no payments are taken. If a paid version is introduced, its refund terms will be published here before any sale.

Other options?

Datemend is not the only tool for this job, and some alternatives are free. According to their own sites on October 18, 2026: GetSnapBack (free, open source, runs on your computer), exportsnap (open source), and DateBack (describes itself as free, desktop app for Mac and Windows). There are also paid tools. We have not tested any of them, and we do not know which is best for your export. Datemend runs in the browser with nothing to install; we have not compared it feature by feature with the tools above.

Feedback and bug reports

Describe what you saw; do not attach your photos, your export, or your report. The most useful thing is the “What was found” summary with any names or places removed.
